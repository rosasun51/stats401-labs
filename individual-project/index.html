Visualization Critique & Redesign

STATS 401 Individual Project · Life expectancy, 1543–2023 · Our World in Data (UN WPP / HMD / Riley, CC BY 4.0)

1 · The Original Visualization

Source: Our World in Data — "Life expectancy" (line chart; country data 1950–2023, selected long-run estimates from 1543).

Data: period life expectancy at birth (years) for World, continents, and ~200 countries, compiled from Riley (2005), Zijdeman et al. (2015), Human Mortality Database (2024) and UN World Population Prospects (2024).

Intended message: life expectancy has roughly doubled worldwide since 1900, with persistent gaps between regions. Audience: general public and students. Tasks: compare places, see trends and convergence, find outliers.

Strengths

  • Exceptional long-run data (estimates back to 1543) on honest, zero-based axes — rare and valuable.
  • Clean default view: World + five continents, distinct colors, direct end labels — the headline story is readable at a glance.
  • Hovering on the source site pops up exact values for any point.

Weaknesses

  • The default view aggregates away country inequality: the "Asia" line averages Japan (≈84 yrs) and Afghanistan (≈62) — the most interesting stories (country gaps, catch-up) are invisible.
  • The path to countries is broken: adding them means picking from a 200+ entry flat list, one at a time; past a handful of lines, overplotting starts and the 20-color cycle repeats hues, so lines become untrackable.
  • No focus or grouping aids: every entity has equal visual weight, there are no end labels once you add countries, and deltas ("how much did X gain since 1950?") must be estimated by eye.

2 · My Redesign — A Focusable Country Explorer (D3.js v7)

Same chart type, same data — but organized for the country-level story: lines are colored by continent; hover a line to keep it bright while everything else fades, with a tooltip showing its exact value and gain since 1950; hover/click a continent chip to spotlight a whole region; Top-N and search manage the 200-country overplotting problem. The World line stays as a dashed reference.

Note: end labels appear when ≤25 countries are shown (otherwise they collide); use hover/search for any country. Continent colors are mapped in-page from ISO country codes (OWID CSVs carry no Continent column); unrecognized economies land in gray “Other”.

3 · What Improved

  • Continent color + group dimming — hovering "Africa" keeps only African countries bright; regional gaps (e.g. Nigeria vs. South Africa) become visible without adding lines one by one.
  • Focus & fade — hovering China leaves it crisp and dims 200 lines to a ghost layer; the tooltip adds the number at the hovered year and its change since 1950.
  • Overplotting under control — Top-10/20/50/All ranking plus instant search replace the flat 200-entry list.

Trade-offs: the Top-N cutoff is a choice, not a fact (switch to All anytime); with many lines shown, reading still relies on focus mode; continent aggregates are dropped in favor of countries plus a World reference.
