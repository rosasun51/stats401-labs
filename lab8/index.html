📖 Corpus: DKU Undergraduate Bulletin

This analysis uses the Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction Bulletin (synthetic excerpt for visualization practice). The document was split into 107 meaningful passages (paragraphs / policy blocks) preserving chapter, section, and page metadata. Text was cleaned, deduplicated, and processed through TF-IDF vectorization + SVD to generate pseudo-semantic embeddings, then projected to 2D via PCA. KMeans clustering identified 8 semantic topics.

🔍 Semantic Findings

1. What are the major semantic topics?

The 8 clusters reveal distinct themes: Registration (add/drop, course enrollment), Grading (GPA, incomplete grades), Curriculum (general education, capstones), Majors (declarations, requirements), Graduation (degree applications, honors), Integrity (honor code, sanctions), Study Away (programs, transfer credits), and Support (advising, wellness, careers). Finance and Campus Life form smaller but coherent clusters.

2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

Registration and Support topics bleed across boundaries. For example, registration-related language appears not only in the Registration section but also in Academic Policies (holds) and Student Support (advising before registration). Similarly, financial aid discussions bridge Financial Information and Student Support (advising).

3. Which sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

Academic Policies is the most diverse, spanning Registration, Grading, and Integrity topics. The matrix shows this section has high counts in three different topic columns. In contrast, Study Away is highly focused, with nearly all passages clustering in one topic.

4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

The bridge passages reveal cross-section semantic similarity. For example, the passage about "registration holds related to academic integrity violations" (Academic Policies → Registration) sits near Integrity cluster passages in the embedding space. Similarly, study-away transfer credit passages align with Graduation requirements, reflecting shared bureaucratic language.

5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?

Passages discussing financial aid implications of course load changes (in Student Support → Advising) appear closer to Finance cluster points than to other Support passages. Likewise, the honor code applying to study away (Academic Integrity → Honor Code) sits at the edge of the Study Away topic region, making it visually distinct from its section peers.

6. Search: "credit" — one region or several?

Searching for "credit" highlights passages across multiple semantic regions: Study Away (transfer credits), Degree Requirements (major credit minimums), Registration (credit hour limits), and Graduation (degree credit completion). This confirms that "credit" is a polysemous concept in the bulletin, carrying different meanings in different contexts — a finding that keyword search alone would not reveal as clearly.

🎨 Design Description

I generated pseudo-semantic embeddings using TF-IDF + TruncatedSVD (50 dimensions) as a lightweight alternative to sentence-transformers, then projected to 2D with PCA for the semantic map. KMeans (k=8) clustered passages, and clusters were labeled by majority section and top TF-IDF terms. The semantic map encodes position as semantic similarity, color as topic, and size as passage length. The Topic × Section matrix uses a blue sequential color scale where darker cells indicate more passages. The two views are coordinated: clicking a matrix cell filters the semantic map to highlight only passages from that section-topic intersection, and clicking a map point highlights its corresponding matrix cell. Search, section/topic filters, zoom/pan, and nearest-neighbor exploration provide multiple entry points into the corpus.
