Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization with D3

2025 Nominal GDP — Choropleth vs. Cartogram

📁 1. Dataset Description

This lab visualizes the 50 largest economies by nominal GDP in 2025 (data/lab9_gdp_2025_top50.csv). The figures are billions of U.S. dollars at current prices, based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025 nominal-GDP series (IMF WEO observations may include staff estimates and can later be revised).

Variables: iso3 (ISO-3 geographic identifier) • country (economy name) • gdp_2025_billion_usd (2025 nominal GDP) • rank (rank among the included economies).

Method: the GDP table is joined to a world-country GeoJSON using the shared ISO-3 identifier. Countries outside the top-50 file are shown in a distinct neutral dark gray as no data — not as zero GDP.

🗺️ 2. 2025 GDP Choropleth — Color = GDP

Each country keeps its true geographic boundary; color encodes 2025 nominal GDP. Because GDP is extremely right-skewed (the U.S. ≈ $30,500B vs. Qatar ≈ $240B, a >100× gap), a log-transformed color scale is used: a linear scale would paint all but the top handful of economies in nearly identical pale shades, while the log scale preserves rank-order and spreads the mid-sized and small economies across the full color range.

Darker/bluer → larger GDP (log scale) • dark gray = no data (outside top 50). Scroll to zoom, drag to pan. Hover a country to highlight it in both maps.

🌐 3. 2025 GDP Cartogram — Area = GDP

A cartogram deliberately re-distorts geography: each country's area is made proportional to its 2025 GDP, not its land area. Large economies (U.S., China) swell; large-but-poorer economies (Russia, Canada, Australia) shrink. Shapes and positions become approximate — hover to identify countries and see them highlighted in the choropleth too.

Country area is proportional to 2025 nominal GDP. Only the 50 economies with data are shown.

⚖️ 4. Design and Comparison

Choropleth encoding

Position → geographic location • Shape → original boundary • Color → GDP. Emphasizes where economic output is.

Cartogram encoding

Position → approximate geography • Shape → distorted • Area → GDP. Emphasizes how much each economy is.

In the choropleth, the most prominent countries are large dark ones — the United States, China, and the big European economies — because color intensity acts within true boundaries. In the cartogram, prominence shifts almost entirely to the United States and China, which balloon to dominate the map, while Canada, Russia, Australia, and Brazil collapse to a fraction of their land area; mid-weight economies such as Germany, India, and Japan expand markedly.

The choropleth preserves accurate geography: relative position, shape, adjacency, and land area all stay intact, so viewers can locate an economy and reason about regional context. The cartogram sacrifices all of these — positions drift, borders bend, and area no longer means land. Accordingly, the choropleth best supports spatial questions (“How is GDP distributed across Europe?”, “Which neighbors are large economies?”), while the cartogram best supports magnitude questions (“How big is country X relative to Y?”, “What share of the top-50 total does each hold?”).

Moving the GDP variable from color to area changes perception substantially. Color is easy to rank but hard to quantify, so the choropleth understates how dominant the U.S. and China really are; area is a far stronger quantitative channel, and the cartogram makes that duopoly — and the long tail of small economies — immediately visceral, at the cost of the geographic intuition the choropleth provides. Neither map is universally better: the right choice depends on whether the task is geographic pattern finding or quantitative comparison.
