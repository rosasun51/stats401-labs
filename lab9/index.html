🌍 Dataset: 2025 Nominal GDP (Top 50)

This dataset contains the 50 largest economies by nominal GDP in 2025, based on IMF World Economic Outlook estimates. Values are in billions of current U.S. dollars. Countries outside the top 50 are shown as missing data (dark background), not zero. The data are joined with a world country boundary map (Natural Earth 110m) using ISO-3 codes.

📊 Design Comparison

1. Most visually prominent countries: In the choropleth, the United States and China stand out through deep blue color saturation. Russia, Canada, and Australia also draw attention due to their large geographic area, despite moderate GDP. In the cartogram, the U.S. and China dominate through sheer size — they occupy dramatically more space than other nations. Small but wealthy countries like Switzerland, Singapore, and the UAE become visibly larger, while large-but-poorer countries shrink.

2. Countries that change size dramatically: India grows substantially in the cartogram, reflecting its #5 GDP rank. Russia, Canada, and Australia shrink significantly because their geographic area far exceeds their economic output. Germany and Japan remain relatively similar in both views. Qatar, Singapore, and UAE expand from tiny specks to clearly visible shapes.

3–4. Geographic information preserved vs. distorted: The choropleth preserves accurate geographic shapes, relative positions, and area proportions. This makes it easy to identify where countries are and compare regional patterns (e.g., Europe's density of high-GDP nations). The cartogram distorts shape, area, and relative position. Neighboring countries may no longer touch; familiar outlines become unrecognizable. What is gained is a direct visual comparison of economic magnitude.

5–6. Tasks each map supports: The choropleth is better for questions like "Which region has the highest GDP?" or "What is the geographic distribution of wealth?" The cartogram is better for "How large is China's economy compared to Germany's?" or "Which top-10 economy is smallest geographically?"

7. Perception shift: Switching from color to area encoding fundamentally changes how we perceive economic size. In the choropleth, Russia looks economically significant because it is dark and huge. In the cartogram, Russia's shrinkage reveals that its economy is modest relative to its territory. Conversely, small rich nations that were barely visible in the choropleth become impossible to ignore in the cartogram. Area encoding feels more "honest" for magnitude comparison, while color encoding feels more familiar for geographic pattern recognition.
